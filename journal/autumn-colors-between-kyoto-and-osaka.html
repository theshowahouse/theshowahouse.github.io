KANSAI AUTUMN GUIDE · AUGUST 2026

Stay Between Kyoto and Osaka for the Best Autumn Colors

A practical guide to Kyoto autumn colors, Osaka autumn colors, Uji, Nara, and quieter local day trips across Kansai.

Kyoto autumn colors may be the best-known reason to visit Kansai in fall, but the season does not end at Kyoto's temple gardens. Osaka autumn colors fill castle grounds, waterfalls, and spacious parks; Nara pairs ancient temples with deer beneath the changing trees; and Uji offers a quieter riverside day of Zen gardens and tea.

Instead of asking whether to stay in Kyoto or Osaka, consider a different rhythm: stay between Kyoto and Osaka and travel outward from one base. You can start early for a quiet Kyoto morning, spend another day among Osaka's wider landscapes, and choose Uji or Nara when you want to slow down—all without packing your suitcase again.

This guide brings those possibilities together, with practical day trips across Kyoto, Osaka, Uji, and Nara, hidden autumn spots near Kyoto, and an easy three-day itinerary that leaves room for weather and changing foliage conditions.

Kyoto Autumn Highlights

Tofukuji is one of Kyoto's classic autumn sights. The view from Tsutenkyo Bridge spreads across a valley of maples, creating layers of red, orange, and gold below the temple buildings. Arrive close to opening time, avoid weekends if your schedule allows, and leave extra time for queues during peak color.

Kiyomizu-dera combines red maples, a hillside temple, and broad views across Kyoto. Morning is best for a calmer visit to the wooden stage and the surrounding Higashiyama streets; late afternoon brings softer light, but usually larger crowds.

Eikando is loved for densely layered maples around its halls and pond, with reflections and framed views appearing as you move through the grounds. Visit early in the daytime for a quieter experience. Evening illuminations are beautiful, but peak nights can be particularly crowded.

Nanzenji rewards visitors with a different kind of Kyoto autumn walk. Its immense Sanmon gate rises above an approach lined with red maples, while the historic brick arches of the Suirokaku Aqueduct create an unexpected contrast with the temple grounds. There is more space to wander than at Kiyomizu-dera or Tofukuji, especially on a weekday morning, so it can feel calmer even during the autumn season.

Arashiyama offers a broader landscape: autumn color across the mountains, the Katsura River, temple gardens, and walking routes beyond the best-known bamboo grove. Arrive before the day-trip crowds, then continue past Togetsukyo Bridge into the quieter temple area rather than spending the whole visit on the main street.

Autumn timing changes with elevation and weather, so do not build the entire trip around one predicted peak date. Check official foliage updates shortly before you travel and keep two or three possible locations in your plan.

Osaka Autumn Highlights

Osaka autumn leaves feel different from Kyoto's temple scenes. The spaces are often broader, the atmosphere more casual, and it is easy to combine autumn color with museums, city views, food, or a gentle hike.

Osaka Castle Park is the easiest choice for a city day. Ginkgo and other seasonal trees frame the castle and its stone walls, while the large grounds give you room to walk even when central Osaka is busy. Morning light is especially pleasant, and arriving from Temmabashi lets you approach through the park rather than beginning at the busiest entrance.

Minoh Falls is the nature-focused option. A roughly three-kilometre walking route follows the river from Minoh Station toward the waterfall, with maple trees, small shops, and places to pause along the way. Start in the morning, wear comfortable shoes, and allow more time than the distance suggests—especially on fine autumn weekends.

Expo '70 Commemorative Park is a spacious alternative for families or anyone who prefers an easy park day. Its Japanese Garden and Natural and Cultural Gardens bring together autumn foliage, open lawns, and the unmistakable Tower of the Sun. Allow at least half a day, check opening days before travelling, and plan for multiple train connections.

Nara in Autumn

Nara Park brings together two of the season's most memorable sights: autumn leaves and the deer that roam freely through the park. Red maples and yellow ginkgo trees create a beautiful backdrop, but the atmosphere feels less like visiting a single viewpoint and more like wandering through a wide, living landscape.

The area around Todaiji is especially rewarding in autumn. You can approach the temple through the park, see deer beneath the changing trees, and continue toward Nigatsudo for a quieter walk and views across Nara.

Nara has a slower rhythm than central Kyoto or Osaka. Give yourself time to walk without a strict checklist, pause when the light changes, and enjoy the combination of temples, parkland, and everyday encounters with the deer.

Uji in Autumn

Uji is a rewarding change of pace between busier days in Kyoto and Osaka. The Uji River, temple approaches, and low wooded hills take on softer autumn color, while the compact center makes it easy to explore without rushing between distant sights.

Begin at Byodoin, where the Phoenix Hall and pond form one of Japan's most recognizable historic scenes. Continue across the river for tea shops, riverside paths, and the walk toward Koshoji. Uji is also one of the best places to pause over matcha rather than treating lunch as another item on a sightseeing checklist.

Travel Without Changing Hotels

Changing hotels takes more time than the train ride alone. Packing, checking out, storing luggage, finding the next property, and checking in again can easily consume the relaxed hours that make autumn travel enjoyable.

One base also gives you flexibility. If rain is forecast for Kyoto, move the temple day and visit an urban museum instead. If the colors are peaking in Nara or Uji, change direction that morning without reorganizing your luggage.

The same plan can help travelers comparing day trips from Kyoto with day trips from Osaka: instead of repeatedly crossing the region from one edge, begin near the middle and choose the best direction each morning.

A simple three-day plan could look like this:

Day 1 — Kyoto: Begin at Tofukuji early, then continue to Kiyomizu-dera and Higashiyama. Choose two main sights and give them enough time rather than chasing every famous viewpoint.

Day 2 — Osaka: Walk through Osaka Castle Park, then continue into central Osaka for lunch, museums, or evening city views. If you prefer forests to city streets, make Minoh Falls the focus of the day instead.

Day 3 — Uji or Nara: Choose Uji for Byodoin, the river, and matcha, or Nara for Todaiji, parkland, and deer beneath the autumn trees. Our detailed Uji guide shows how to make Uji a calm day trip rather than a rushed stop.

If you have a fourth day, visit whichever of Uji or Nara you did not choose. A Nara autumn day and an Uji autumn day each create a different experience—deer and ancient temples in one direction, riverside paths and tea culture in the other.

Explore Kansai by Keihan Railway

This is where the geography becomes useful. Kuzuha Station, the nearest station to The Showa House, sits on the Keihan Railway between central Osaka and eastern Kyoto. Rather than returning to Kyoto Station for every day trip, you can use the Keihan corridor as the backbone of the journey.

Northbound trains lead toward Tofukuji, Fushimi Inari, and Kiyomizu-gojo for Kiyomizu-dera and Higashiyama. Southbound trains reach Temmabashi and Kyobashi, both useful for Osaka Castle. Uji requires one straightforward change at Chushojima onto the Keihan Uji Line.

At a glance, the most useful routes are:

  • Tofukuji — Direct on the Keihan Main Line
  • Fushimi Inari — Direct on the Keihan Main Line
  • Kiyomizu-dera — Keihan to Kiyomizu-gojo, then a walk uphill
  • Uji and Byodoin — Easy transfer at Chushojima to the Keihan Uji Line
  • Osaka Castle — Direct to Temmabashi, or transfer at Kyobashi for nearby JR stations
  • Iwashimizu Hachimangu — Two local stops from Kuzuha, then the cable car

The result is not that every Kansai sight sits on one railway, but that one clear north–south route can organize much of the trip. It reduces daily route planning and makes it easier to decide each morning whether the best conditions are in Kyoto, Osaka, or Uji.

Not every attraction is beside a Keihan platform: allow for walks from the station, and remember that Arashiyama, Minoh, Nara, and Expo '70 Commemorative Park require other lines or connections. Even so, the railway makes it unusually simple to combine Kyoto, Osaka, and Uji without changing hotels.

Hidden Autumn Spots Near The Showa House

The famous gardens deserve their reputation, but autumn in Kansai becomes more personal when you add a place that is not on every first-time itinerary. These three outings are quieter alternatives for a flexible afternoon or an unhurried final day.

Uji River & Koshoji Temple

From Kuzuha, take a Keihan limited express toward Kyoto and change at Chushojima for the Uji Line; the journey usually takes around 30–40 minutes, depending on the connection. Beyond Byodoin, cross toward the east bank of the Uji River and follow the approach to Koshoji. The maple-lined slope is called Kotosaka, or “Koto Slope,” and its stone walls and autumn canopy create a peaceful entrance to the Zen temple.

Combine the walk with Byodoin, the riverbanks, and a stop for Uji matcha. It is one of the easiest ways to experience Uji autumn without turning the day into a race between attractions.

Iwashimizu Hachimangu

Iwashimizu-hachimangu Station is a short ride—two local stops—from Kuzuha. Beside the station, the Iwashimizu-hachimangu-sando-cable carries visitors up Mount Otokoyama, turning the journey to the shrine into part of the experience.

At the top, forest paths, views from Mount Otokoyama, and the vermilion shrine buildings feel far removed from the major sightseeing routes. The cable ride, wooded mountain setting, and quieter atmosphere make this a rewarding experience for overseas visitors who want to look beyond the best-known temple districts.

Kuzuha Mirror Pond Park (Shimin no Mori and Kyoden Pond)

Shimin no Mori, officially Kyoden Pond Green Space, can be reached on foot or by local Keihan Bus from the Kuzuha area. It is a small community park arranged around Kyoden Pond, with garden paths, trees, and places to pause beside the water.

On still autumn days, seasonal color can be reflected on the pond. This is not a famous sightseeing destination, and that is precisely its appeal: you may see local families, walkers, and birdwatchers rather than tour groups. It offers a quiet glimpse of everyday life in Japan between larger day trips.

Plan an Autumn Journey Across Kansai

You do not have to choose between Kyoto's temple gardens and Osaka's parks and forest trails. The most memorable route may combine both, then slow the pace with deer beneath the trees in Nara, a riverside afternoon in Uji, or a neighborhood park that never appears on a “must-see” list.
