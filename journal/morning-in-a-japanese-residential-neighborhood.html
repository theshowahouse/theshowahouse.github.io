A Peaceful Morning Walk in a Japanese Neighborhood

An ordinary morning in a Japanese residential neighborhood.

From Taka’s Notebook

When I travel, some days begin early. If there is somewhere I want to see before it becomes busy, I am happy to leave in the morning. From The Showa House, a day at Fushimi Inari might begin that way.

But I do not think every morning needs to begin like that. If I have nowhere I need to reach early, I sometimes walk around the neighbourhood near where I am staying. If I am tired, I can simply rest instead.

On a walk, I might see people going to work or school, someone walking a dog, someone sweeping in front of a home, or a small shop I did not notice the day before. There is nothing arranged for visitors. I enjoy famous sights too, but I also like seeing these completely ordinary mornings when I travel abroad. Kuzuha is this kind of place.

There Is No Special Walking Route Here

The Showa House is in an ordinary residential neighbourhood in Hirakata. There is no famous building you need to see on a morning walk, and there is no special sightseeing course to follow.

People walk to the station. Bicycles pass. Someone takes care of a home or garden, and the scenery changes a little with the seasons. That is about it.

That is exactly the kind of place I often enjoy walking through when I travel. It is not scenery prepared for visitors. It is simply part of someone’s everyday life.

Respect the Neighbourhood

This is not a tourist attraction; it is a place where people live. Keep your voice reasonable, stay aware of bicycles and cars on narrow streets, and avoid photographing people or private homes without permission.

I Also Like Visiting the Same Supermarket Again

The supermarket is a different part of this habit. The nearby supermarkets do not open early, but when I stay somewhere for several days, I often visit the same local supermarket more than once.

The first time, I am usually looking at unfamiliar products and trying to understand the shop. I notice seasonal fruit, bento, prepared foods, and what local residents seem to be buying for dinner. Near closing time, some bento and prepared foods may have discount stickers on them. That is simply another ordinary part of supermarket life. Even the same shop can feel different depending on the time of day.

Some travellers may think that visiting the same shop twice is a waste of travel time. I feel almost the opposite. When I return another day, the same shop already feels slightly more familiar, and the unfamiliar town does too. I enjoy that feeling when I travel.

Nothing Special Has to Happen

If you have about 30 minutes on a morning when there is no need to hurry, you might walk around the neighbourhood. You could head toward Kuzuha Station, stop at a convenience store, or follow a street that looks interesting.

If nothing interesting happens, that is completely fine. The walk does not need to become another sightseeing activity where you feel required to discover something. Seeing what an ordinary residential neighbourhood looks like in the morning is enough.

Morning and Evening

On a day when you want to reach Fushimi Inari or somewhere else early, go directly to Kuzuha Station. On a morning without a fixed plan, you might walk through the neighbourhood or stop at a convenience store before continuing to the station. Both are good ways to begin a day in Kyoto, Osaka, Uji, or Nara.

In the evening, you return to Kuzuha and might stop at a local supermarket. You see people shopping for dinner, buy something if you want, and walk back to The Showa House. After a few days, the same streets and shops may begin to feel a little more familiar.

There may be mornings when you leave early for a famous place, and mornings when nothing needs to happen quickly. If you are tired, resting at the house is also a good choice. The point is not to make yourself follow the same routine every day.

If you have an unhurried morning, a short walk without a particular destination can show you the ordinary Japan where people are simply living their lives. Later that day, you may visit the Japan that people travel around the world to see. I enjoy both. An ordinary morning that seemed like nothing at the time may surprisingly become one of the things you remember after returning home.
