From The Showa House: Where We Like to Eat

A few local places our family genuinely enjoys

From Taka’s Notebook

Guests sometimes ask me, “Where do you eat around here?”

When they do, I do not search for a famous restaurant to suggest. I tell them about places where my family and I actually go for an ordinary meal.

I do not think every meal while travelling needs to be special. Eating at a place that local families use in their everyday lives can also be an enjoyable part of a trip.

These are a few places near The Showa House that our family genuinely visits. Some are a short walk away, while others are easier to reach by car.

Ajisai (Takoyaki & Okonomiyaki)

We go to Ajisai for takoyaki, okonomiyaki, and yakisoba. It is a casual choice when we feel like eating the flour-based dishes often associated with Osaka.

For a guest trying this kind of food for the first time, the Okonomi Set is a practical choice. It includes okonomiyaki, takoyaki, and yakisoba, so you can try all three in one meal.

Ten (Soba Restaurant)

Ten is a soba restaurant where we actually go for lunch. It is open only during lunchtime, so this is somewhere we choose when we want a straightforward midday meal rather than an evening out.

We often order the Tempura Zaru Soba Set, with chilled soba and freshly fried tempura.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Nepal Kitchen Kumari (Nepalese Curry Restaurant)

When we are in the mood for curry, this is where we like to go. The staff always make us feel welcome. It can also be a comfortable choice for a traveller who has been eating Japanese food every day and feels like having something different.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Wabisuke · Kuzuha Station (Japanese Restaurant)

Wabisuke is a Japanese restaurant inside Kuzuha Station. It is also an izakaya, but our family uses it for lunch as well. Because it is inside the station, it is convenient before going out or after returning to Kuzuha.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Ohno (Soba Restaurant)

Ohno is another soba restaurant our family uses. It is a little way up the hill from The Showa House and has a wide range of dishes to choose from.

We sometimes go here when Ten is closed. It is not a matter of one restaurant being the only choice; some days we go to one, and some days to the other. When you live in an area, a few places gradually become your usual restaurants in this way.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Hama Sushi (Conveyor Belt Sushi)

Of course, coming to Japan can be a good opportunity to visit a special sushi restaurant. But if you are also curious about the kind of sushi many people in Japan eat as an ordinary family meal, a conveyor-belt chain such as Hama Sushi can be interesting too. Our family uses it in exactly that way.

The prices are relatively approachable, and the menu includes not only sushi but also side dishes and desserts. It is a different kind of meal from high-end sushi, but it is part of everyday dining in Japan.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Sakura-tei (Japanese-style Western Restaurant)

We go to Sakura-tei when we feel like eating hamburger steak. It serves yoshoku, Japanese-style Western food: dishes that developed in Japan from Western influences and became familiar everyday meals here.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Hakata Tempura Yamaya (Tempura Restaurant)

Hakata Tempura Yamaya is one of the places our family personally likes. Freshly fried tempura arrives one piece at a time, so you can eat each piece while it is still hot. You can also enjoy mentaiko, spicy cod roe, with the meal.

📍 View on Google Maps ↗

Places That Are Part of Our Everyday Life

I did not search for these restaurants so that I could put them in a travel guide. They are places our family chooses in an ordinary way: “Let’s have soba today,” “I feel like curry,” or “Hama Sushi sounds good for sushi.”

Visiting a famous restaurant can be fun when you travel. But I also think it can be interesting to eat once at a place a local family uses regularly. If you find one restaurant that becomes your own favourite during your stay, that may become a small memory of the trip too.

Some Evenings, I Just Bring Dinner Home

After walking around Kyoto or Osaka all day, you may not want to eat out every evening. Some evenings I want a restaurant; on others, I am happy not to look for one more place. When I am tired while travelling, I also like to buy something, return to where I am staying, and eat at my own pace.

Around Kuzuha Station, you can find bento, prepared foods, supermarkets, and other options to take back to The Showa House. I have collected some of the places that are useful on those evenings in Where to Buy Bento & Groceries Near The Showa House.
