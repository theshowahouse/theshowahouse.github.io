THE HOUSE · JULY 2026

What Is a Showa House?

Not a museum or a grand historic residence, but an ordinary Japanese home whose everyday character has quietly become rare.

“Showa” refers to the Japanese era that lasted from 1926 to 1989. It was a long period of enormous change: Japan moved through war, recovery, rapid economic growth and the arrival of many things that shaped modern daily life.

A Showa house is not one fixed architectural style. The phrase is often used more loosely for homes that still carry the materials, proportions and atmosphere of everyday life from that era.

An ordinary home

The Showa House was the home of our great-aunt, who lived to be 100 years old. She grew seasonal vegetables in the garden and made kimono, building a quiet life here one day at a time.

Because she cherished the house and made few major changes, much of its original character remains: tatami rooms, wooden frames, patterned glass, familiar furniture and the small signs of a home that was lived in rather than designed as an exhibit.

What was once ordinary has quietly become something rare.

Why these homes feel different

Many older Japanese homes have been rebuilt as lifestyles, safety standards and ideas of comfort changed. That change is natural, but it also means that a particular kind of everyday atmosphere is disappearing.

At The Showa House, the value is not luxury or historical grandeur. It is the chance to stay inside a real home, sleep on tatami, notice the soft light through old glass and experience a slower rhythm after a day in Kyoto or Osaka.

A home, not a hotel

We preserve the house because its ordinariness matters. It offers a small window into the way many Japanese families once lived—and into a kind of warmth that cannot be recreated simply by decorating a new building in a retro style.

That is the meaning behind our words: Not a hotel. A home to remember.
