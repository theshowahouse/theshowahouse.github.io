What It's Like to Sleep on a Traditional Japanese Futon

For someone trying it for the first time, sleeping on tatami may sound like sleeping directly on the floor. The experience is a little different.

In Japan, we remove our shoes at the entrance before stepping inside a home. Outdoor shoes are not worn in tatami rooms, so the place where the futon is laid is separate from the dirt of the street.

If you are visiting Japan for the first time, you may wonder whether a futon means sleeping directly on a hard floor. A futon is bedding designed to be laid out for the night and folded away during the day, allowing a tatami room to be used flexibly.

A Japanese way to sleep

The exact setup varies between homes and traditional inns. At The Showa House, the futon is placed over a comfortable mattress about 10 centimetres thick, with freshly washed sheets and a soft down comforter.

It is different from sleeping in a bed—not better or worse, simply a different way to sleep, and one that has long been part of everyday life in Japan.

The sounds of the house

In the morning, soft light filters through the shoji screens. The sounds around the house also change with the seasons.

In early summer, frogs can sometimes be heard in the evening. Later in summer, mornings begin with the chorus of cicadas. On rainy days, you may hear the sound of rain falling on the roof.

None of these sounds are arranged as part of an attraction. They are simply part of everyday life in this house.

The house is not silent. It is quiet enough for you to notice the season.

A restful night

Many of our guests tell us that they slept very well here.

I feel that sleep in this house has a comfort that lets the body slowly settle in, almost as though it is becoming part of the space itself.

Everyone experiences a place differently, of course, but perhaps this is one of the quiet charms of the house.

Sleeping on a futon is one way to experience the practical design of a traditional Japanese house. The Showa House offers this experience in Hirakata, with convenient day-trip access to Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, and Uji.
