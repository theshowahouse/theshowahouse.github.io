Why Is Hirakata a Good Base for Kyoto and Osaka?

Choosing a place for the journey you actually plan to take.

From Taka’s Notebook

I do not imagine that many international travellers begin planning a trip to Japan by saying, “I want to stay in Hirakata.” Hirakata is not Kyoto. It is not central Osaka. Kuzuha is not a famous international sightseeing destination.

So why would someone choose it as a place to stay?

When I travel, I choose accommodation by asking which location makes the trip I am planning most convenient. I do not usually choose a place simply because the city is famous.

If most of my trip is in Kyoto, staying in Kyoto makes sense. If I mainly want to enjoy Osaka, I would stay in Osaka. But if I plan to spend several days visiting both, I begin to wonder whether staying somewhere between them might work.

Hirakata enters the plan through that practical question. The ordinary Japanese life around Kuzuha is something you encounter afterwards, simply because this is where you return each day.

Start with the Itinerary, Not the Name of the City

For a Kyoto-centred trip, Kyoto is usually the more convenient base. This is especially true if you want to walk through central Kyoto early every morning. For a trip centred on Osaka, Osaka is the more natural choice, particularly if you expect to stay out late there most evenings.

The Showa House is not the right base for every traveller, and I do not want to pretend that it is. The idea of staying in Kuzuha works best when Kyoto and Osaka both matter to the itinerary over several days.

Kuzuha Station is on the Keihan Main Line. From the same station, you can travel in the Kyoto direction one day and the Osaka direction the next. Uji can also fit naturally into another day.

I am not saying that Hirakata is the best place for every destination. I am saying that the location can make sense for one particular kind of trip: several days divided between Kyoto and Osaka.

Hirakata Can Be the Place You Return To

In the morning, you might leave Kuzuha Station for Kyoto. You walk through Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, and Gion, then take the Keihan Railway back in the evening.

At Kuzuha Station, people are coming home from work. Students are returning from school, and other people are buying something for dinner. You spent the day in Kyoto as a visitor; now you have returned to an ordinary part of Japan where daily life is continuing.

The next day, you can leave from the same station in the opposite direction for Osaka. On another day, you might go to Uji. Hirakata is not replacing any of these destinations. Its role is to be the place you come back to afterwards.

I would not choose Kuzuha only because I wanted a “local experience.” I would first decide whether the location fits the trip. If it does, the ordinary life around the station becomes an unplanned part of the stay.

Not a Sightseeing District, but Easy to Live In for a Few Days

Kuzuha is not a sightseeing district, but the area around Kuzuha Station has the things that make an ordinary stay manageable: KUZUHA MALL, supermarkets, convenience stores, restaurants, and pharmacies.

After returning from a day in Kyoto or Osaka, you can buy dinner at a supermarket. If you are tired, you do not have to search for another restaurant; you can take something back to the house and eat there.

After a few days, you begin to know which station exit to use, where the supermarket is, and which road leads home. You have not come to Kuzuha to collect attractions. You have simply developed a small living area around the place where you are staying.

A Small Historical Note

Hirakata also has a light historical connection with travel between Kyoto and Osaka. During the Edo period, Hirakata-shuku developed as a post town on the old Kyokaido road linking the two cities.

The Showa House is in Kuzuha, a different part of present-day Hirakata. Kuzuha itself was not Hirakata-shuku. I mention the history only because I find it interesting that people have travelled through this city between Kyoto and Osaka for a long time.

Travel today is completely different, and I would not turn this into a story that Kuzuha has always been the same kind of base. It is simply a little background to Hirakata’s position between the two cities.

Why Choose a Place Most Travellers Do Not Know?

Not because an unknown place is automatically more interesting, and not because staying outside Kyoto or Osaka is somehow better. I would choose Hirakata only when it makes practical sense for the journey.

Then something else may happen. You enjoy the famous places in Kyoto and Osaka during the day, and in the evening you return to an ordinary Japanese town. A place you had never heard of becomes the place where you know the station exit, the supermarket, and the walk home.

A few days later, when you leave Japan, you may remember Kiyomizu-dera and Dotonbori. But you may also remember the road from Kuzuha Station to the house, or the supermarket where you stopped more than once. I think a trip that leaves room for both kinds of memory can be an interesting one.
