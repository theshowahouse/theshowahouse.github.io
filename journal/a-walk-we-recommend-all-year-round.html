KYOTO DAY TRIP · JULY 2026

A Walk at Sewaritei, Just Two Stops from Kuzuha

A riverside walk for a day when you do not need another full itinerary.

From Taka’s Notebook

When people think of Sewaritei, they usually think of cherry blossoms.

In spring, the long row of trees turns pink and many people come to see it. I like Sewaritei during cherry blossom season too. But the blossoms are not the only reason I suggest this walk to guests.

Sewaritei is near the place where the Kizu, Uji, and Katsura rivers come together and continue as the Yodo River. There is a lot of nature here: rivers, broad riverbanks, open sky, and plants that change with the seasons.

The time you spend here feels completely different from a day among the temples and old streets of central Kyoto. I come here less to see one special thing than simply to walk beside the river.

From Kuzuha Station, it is only two stops on the Keihan Main Line: Kuzuha, Hashimoto, then Iwashimizu-hachimangu Station. From there, Sewaritei is a short walk.

Cherry Blossom Season Is Special—and Busy

When the cherry trees are in bloom, the long riverside path is a special sight. People walk beneath the blossoms, take photographs, and sit down for picnics.

It is also very popular. If you arrive expecting a completely quiet riverbank, the number of people may be a small surprise. That is part of Sewaritei in spring too.

Even when it is busy, the rivers and broad sky make the setting feel very different from the narrow streets around Kyoto’s best-known temples.

I Like It Even Without the Blossoms

After the blossoms are gone, Sewaritei returns to being a fairly ordinary riverbank. Summer is green. In autumn, the colours of the grasses and trees change. Winter brings a quieter, more open landscape. That is about it.

I say that honestly, but I like spending this kind of time when I travel. Seeing one more temple in Kyoto or visiting one more place in Osaka can be enjoyable. If I am staying for several days, however, I am also happy to spend half a day without trying to complete anything.

A walk beside the river can be enough. You can turn back when you feel like it and return to The Showa House early.

If You Still Have Time and Energy

If you would like to continue after the flat riverbank walk, you could also visit Iwashimizu Hachimangu on Mount Otokoyama. The forest and shrine grounds feel quite different from the open riverside below.

You can walk up the mountain, or take the cable car if you are tired or do not feel like climbing. If the day still feels comfortable, you might stop at the Otokoyama viewpoint or have Hashirii Mochi on the way back.

These are options, not a list to complete. If the Sewaritei walk was enough for the day, simply return to the station.

A Smaller Day Is Still a Good Day

I understand why a first visit to Japan includes Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera, and Gion. I recommend those places too. But after several full sightseeing days, it is normal to feel tired.

Sewaritei does not need to become a large day trip. It can be somewhere you visit after a slow morning, or a walk that lets you return to The Showa House by late afternoon. From Iwashimizu-hachimangu Station, the journey home is again two stops: Iwashimizu-hachimangu, Hashimoto, then Kuzuha.

After staying for a few days, arriving at Kuzuha Station may begin to feel a little like coming home. You can buy dinner near the station, at KUZUHA MALL, or at a supermarket, then return to the house and rest in the tatami room.

Some days begin early and are filled with Kyoto. Other days are simply for walking beside a river. I think a trip can have room for both.
