Best Base for Exploring Kyoto and Osaka

A practical way to explore both cities without moving your suitcase.

From Taka’s Notebook

When people plan to visit both Kyoto and Osaka, they often think they need to choose one city or move hotels during the trip.

There is another option: stay in one place between the two cities and use it as a base.

I do not think this is automatically the best choice for everyone. It depends on where you most want to spend your time. But for a multi-day trip that includes both cities, it can be very practical.

I Don’t Really Like Changing Hotels While Traveling

I do not particularly like changing accommodation again and again during a trip.

A hotel-change day usually means packing in the morning, checking out, moving with a suitcase, finding somewhere for the luggage before the next check-in, sightseeing, returning to the new accommodation, checking in, and unpacking again.

If I am doing all of this simply to move from Kyoto to Osaka, I find it a little troublesome. Moving from one accommodation to another becomes one of the main tasks of the day. After checking out, I also have to think about what to do with my large luggage until I can check in to the next place.

I prefer to leave the suitcase where it is, take only what I need for the day, go sightseeing, and come back in the evening. It is not a grand travel philosophy. I simply find it easier.

Staying “Between” Kyoto and Osaka

The Showa House is in Kuzuha, Hirakata, between Kyoto and Osaka. The nearest station is Kuzuha Station on the Keihan Main Line.

Kuzuha is a major stop on the line, and Limited Express trains stop there. For a visitor, the useful point is simple: from the same station, you can travel toward Kyoto one day and toward Osaka the next.

Another day could be Uji. Nara is also possible as a longer day trip. I do not mean that every trip needs all four destinations. The basic idea is simply: today Kyoto, tomorrow Osaka, another day Uji. Same accommodation. No suitcase transfer.

Sometimes Kyoto Is the Better Base

If most of your trip is about Kyoto, I would probably stay in Kyoto. It is convenient if you want to begin exploring central Kyoto very early in the morning.

If Osaka is the main purpose of the trip, staying in Osaka makes sense. Osaka may also be more convenient if you plan to enjoy its nightlife very late every evening.

Stay near the place that matters most to your trip.

But what if Kyoto and Osaka matter equally? That is when staying between them becomes interesting.

Why the Keihan Line Matters

Not every geographical midpoint between Kyoto and Osaka is equally useful. Kuzuha is on the Keihan Main Line, which serves the Fushimi and Higashiyama side of Kyoto, including useful stops for Fushimi Inari, Tofukuji, Gion, and the Kiyomizu area. Trains also run in the other direction toward Osaka.

Rather than thinking, “I am staying somewhere halfway between two cities,” I think of it this way: from the same station, today I travel in the Kyoto direction; tomorrow I travel in the Osaka direction.

If you are planning a Kyoto day, our Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera and Gion route gives one practical example.

Early-Morning Kyoto

I like visiting Kyoto early. Fushimi Inari, for example, can feel very different early in the morning from later in the day.

You can still use The Showa House for an early Kyoto day by leaving early. But if you want to be walking through central Kyoto at six every morning, staying in Kyoto is simply more convenient. The best base depends on the actual itinerary.

The Simple Advantage of One Base

After a few days in one place, you know the road to the station, where the supermarket is, and where to buy something on the way home. Your suitcase is also still where you left it.

I write more about familiarity in How Many Days Do You Need for Kyoto, Osaka and Nara? But here, I mean something much simpler: it is easier.

I prefer to spend as little time as possible during a trip thinking about moving from one accommodation to another and what to do with my luggage.

The Showa House and the History of This Location

The Showa House is a small Japanese home in an ordinary residential neighbourhood in Kuzuha, Hirakata.

There is also a light historical connection to the location. Hirakata-shuku, in what is now central Hirakata, developed as a post town on the old Kyokaido road between Kyoto and Osaka. The Showa House is not in Hirakata-shuku, and Kuzuha itself was not that post town. The connection is simply that people have traveled through this city between Kyoto and Osaka for a very long time.

Transportation is completely different today, but Hirakata’s position between the two cities still matters.

The Showa House is not the most convenient accommodation for every traveler. But it can be an interesting base if you want to visit Kyoto and Osaka, perhaps add Uji or Nara, leave your luggage in one place, and stay several nights in the same home.

So Where Should You Stay?

If Kyoto is the centre of your trip, stay in Kyoto.

If Osaka is the centre of your trip, stay in Osaka.

If you are spending several days exploring both Kyoto and Osaka, consider putting one base somewhere between them.

I prefer traveling light. I would rather spend my time walking around a town than moving a suitcase from one accommodation to another.

When planning a Kyoto-and-Osaka trip, perhaps the question does not always have to be, “Kyoto or Osaka?” There is also, “What about somewhere in between?”
