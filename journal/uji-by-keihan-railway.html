UJI DAY TRIP · JULY 2026

How to Get to Uji by Keihan Railway

Matcha, the Uji River, and an easy local train journey from Hirakata.

From Taka’s Notebook

Many guests at The Showa House tell me that they love matcha.

They drink it in Kyoto, try matcha ice cream, and take home matcha sweets. After welcoming guests from many countries, I have realised how much matcha can become part of someone’s experience of Japan.

So I sometimes ask, “Are you going to Uji?”

In Japan, Uji is a name that many people naturally associate with tea and matcha. Some visitors know matcha and Kyoto very well without knowing much about the town itself. If you already enjoy matcha, I think it is worth visiting the place connected with it.

Getting from Kuzuha to Uji

From Kuzuha Station, take the Keihan Main Line to Chushojima Station.

At Chushojima, transfer to the Keihan Uji Line and continue to its final stop, Uji Station.

There is only one transfer, and you do not need to pass through Kyoto Station. The journey usually takes about 35 minutes.

I like this journey too. The Uji Line is not a sightseeing railway; it is an ordinary local line that people use in everyday life. That simple, everyday quality is part of what I enjoy about it.

Uji Is More Than Matcha

I definitely want guests who love matcha to enjoy some tea in Uji. But tea is not the only reason I like the town. There is Byodoin, the Uji River, the streets lined with tea shops, and Koshoji if you feel like taking a longer walk.

Uji is also the setting of Sound! Euphonium, a Japanese coming-of-age anime about a high school concert band. The town and the Keihan Uji Line appear throughout the story, and fans visit the real locations they recognise from the series as a kind of anime pilgrimage, known in Japanese as seichi junrei.

I am a fan myself. When I walk around Uji, I sometimes recognise a station, a train, or a place beside the river and think, “Oh, I’ve seen this place before.” It adds another small layer of enjoyment.

You do not need to know the anime to enjoy Uji. Is Uji interesting because it became the setting of the series, or did it become such an attractive setting because Uji was already an interesting town? I think it is probably both.

Do Not Plan Every Minute

A natural day might be very simple: visit Byodoin, have some tea, and walk beside the Uji River. If you still feel like walking, you can continue toward Koshoji.

This is not a list you need to complete. If you find a tea shop you like and stay longer than expected, that is perfectly fine. You came all the way to Uji, so take some time to enjoy Uji.

Byodoin, a tea shop, and a walk beside the river can already make a very good day. There is no need to keep moving just because another place appears on a map.

If you would like a longer walk, continue along the river toward Koshoji Temple. Its Kotosaka approach rises gently beneath a canopy of trees and is especially beautiful during the autumn foliage season.

Why I Prefer Going in the Morning

When possible, I like to leave for Uji in the morning. I do not go early so that I can finish sightseeing earlier. I go early so that I do not have to hurry.

Having enough time makes it easier to stay for another bowl of tea, sit beside the river, or continue to Koshoji without watching the clock.

On the Way Home

Take the Keihan Uji Line back to Chushojima, then transfer to the Keihan Main Line toward Kuzuha.

If you still have enough time and energy, you could stop at Iwashimizu Hachimangu on the way home. But if you have spent a relaxed day in Uji and feel tired, just return to The Showa House. You do not need to fill every hour of a trip.

If You Love Matcha, Remember Uji

You can drink matcha in Kyoto, eat matcha ice cream, and find matcha sweets in many places. But visiting Uji, drinking tea there, and walking beside the river gives that interest another dimension.

From The Showa House, it does not need to feel like a major expedition. You can leave Kuzuha in the morning on the Keihan Railway and return to the same house later that day. That is why, when a guest tells me they love matcha, I often ask, “Are you going to Uji?”
