EVERYDAY JAPAN · JULY 2026

Why Stay in a Traditional Japanese House?

Famous sights show you Japan. An ordinary home lets you experience how everyday life in Japan feels.

Many people travel to Japan to visit its famous sights.

The temples of Kyoto.

The food of Osaka.

The deer of Nara.

Of course, they are all wonderful, and we hope every visitor enjoys them.

But when I travel, what I most want to see is something quieter: the ordinary lives of the people who live there.

Staying in a traditional Japanese house can offer that perspective, but it helps to know what to expect. Tatami rooms, futon bedding, sliding doors, and removing shoes are part of the experience. Older homes may also feel different from a modern hotel in sound, temperature, and the way rooms are used.

Not a museum. Not a luxury ryokan.

What we hope you will experience at The Showa House is not something grand or extraordinary.

It is not a museum.

It is not a luxury ryokan.

It is an ordinary Japanese home where my great-aunt once lived.

The sound of a shoji screen sliding open.

The gentle feeling of tatami beneath your feet.

Morning light entering through the windows.

A small garden whose character changes with the seasons.

Each of these things is small. Yet together, they quietly tell the story of everyday life in Japan.

A house made for living

Hotels are designed to make a stay efficient and comfortable.

This house, however, was made for people to live in.

Its marks, stains and scratches are not imperfections to hide. They are signs that someone once called this place home.

History is not only something to observe behind glass. It is something to experience quietly.

Moments that cannot be scheduled

Some guests tell us that they slept more deeply than they expected.

Some sit on the tatami and enjoy a cup of tea.

Others open the window in the evening and listen as the residential neighborhood gradually becomes quiet.

These moments cannot be written into an itinerary.

They simply happen.

Between Kyoto and Osaka

The Showa House is in Hirakata, a city located between Kyoto and Osaka.

During the day, you can explore the famous sights of both cities. In the evening, you can return to a quiet residential neighborhood.

Some guests tell us that this balance became one of the most memorable parts of their journey.

What You May Remember

When you leave Japan, we hope you will remember not only where you went, but also the kind of home you returned to at the end of each day.

The Showa House is one option for travellers who want this local experience while using Hirakata as a base for Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, and Uji.
