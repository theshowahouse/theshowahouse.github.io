How Many Days Do You Need for Kyoto, Osaka and Nara?

Not to see everything, but to leave a little time to become familiar with the place.

From Taka’s Notebook

People sometimes ask me, “How many days do I need for Kyoto, Osaka and Nara?”

Three days can work: one day in Kyoto, one in Osaka, and one in Nara. If you choose the major sights and travel efficiently, that can still be a very enjoyable trip.

But if you ask what I personally recommend, I would say around five days.

This is not because five days is required to see all the attractions in Kyoto and Osaka. Five days is not about seeing more. It is about having enough time to decide that you do not need to see everything.

Three Mornings at Wat Pho

Many years ago, when I was traveling in Bangkok, I went to Wat Pho three days in a row. It was not because I wanted to see the reclining Buddha three days in a row.

What I liked was the journey there. Each morning, I left the same accommodation, walked the same streets, passed through the same market, and continued toward Wat Pho.

On the first day, everything was unfamiliar. On the second, I thought, “Oh, that shop was here yesterday.” The same food-stall owner was there again.

By the third morning, I roughly knew the route. A completely unfamiliar part of Bangkok now contained one road that felt familiar to me.

Nothing dramatic happened. The market had not changed. The streets had not changed. The place had not changed. My relationship with it had.

Five Days Means You Do Not Have to Do Everything

Five days might include a day in Kyoto, a day in Osaka, and a day in Nara. Perhaps you visit Uji or Fushimi. Perhaps you return somewhere you liked. Perhaps one day has no fixed plan.

Five days does not mean you can do everything. It means you do not have to.

If Kyoto interests you more, spend another day there. If you liked somewhere yesterday, go back. If you are tired, do less. That is still travel.

For one possible Kyoto day, I have written about how I would visit Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera and Gion.

Another day might take you to Osaka, with its busy streets, bright signs, and a completely different energy.

Nara offers temple paths, old trees, and deer. Uji can be another day, with tea, the river, and time to sit.

One Place, Different Days

I like staying in one place for several days rather than changing hotels constantly. Each morning, I leave through the same entrance and walk the same road to the station. Each evening, I return to the same station and walk the same road home.

After a few days, I know, “That turn leads to the station.” “That supermarket is useful on the way home.” “That convenience store is open early.”

This is not a tourist attraction. It is simply a small temporary living area that becomes familiar during the trip, much like the road I walked to Wat Pho.

Kyoto, Osaka, Nara—and a Place to Return To

The Showa House is in Hirakata, between Kyoto and Osaka. From the same home base, guests can spend different days in Kyoto, Osaka, Nara, and Uji.

Each evening, they return to the same ordinary residential neighbourhood and the same house. They walk back from the station, perhaps buy something at the local supermarket, take off their shoes at the entrance, and relax in the tatami room. The next morning, they leave from the same house again.

This is not the only way to travel. It is simply the kind of travel I personally enjoy: different days, but one familiar place to return to.

If you have time, that routine might include a morning walk around the neighbourhood or learning what it feels like to sleep on a traditional Japanese futon.

Why I Say Five Days

So, how many days do you need for Kyoto, Osaka and Nara?

Three days can work. If you can stay longer, that is also wonderful. But when someone asks what I personally recommend, I say around five days.

After walking the same road to Wat Pho for three mornings, a completely unfamiliar part of Bangkok had begun to contain a road I knew. That feeling was different from adding one more famous attraction to my itinerary.

Five days is not enough to see everything. That is not why I recommend it.

I recommend five days because it leaves a little time to become familiar with the place.
