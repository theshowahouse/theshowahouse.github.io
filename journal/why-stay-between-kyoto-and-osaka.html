KANSAI TRAVEL GUIDE · JULY 2026

Why Stay Between Kyoto and Osaka?

Explore Kyoto and Osaka by day, then return to an ordinary Japanese neighbourhood in the evening.

From Taka’s Notebook

There is a practical reason to stay between Kyoto and Osaka, but I think there is another reason too: you can spend several days in an ordinary Japanese town that is not a famous tourist destination.

The Showa House is in Kuzuha, Hirakata. Hirakata is not Kyoto, and it is not central Osaka. Kuzuha is not a major international sightseeing destination. It is an ordinary place where people live.

I find that interesting because ordinary local life is something I look for when I travel.

I Like Seeing Ordinary Life When I Travel

I like famous sightseeing places. If I visit another country, of course I want to see the places that are important there.

But I am equally interested in walking through residential streets, visiting local supermarkets, and going into small shops used by local residents. I notice what people normally eat, where they shop, what kind of homes they return to, and what an ordinary morning or evening looks like.

I am not looking for a special cultural performance. I am simply curious about how people live.

Kyoto by Day, Kuzuha by Night

A guest might leave The Showa House in the morning, spend the day sightseeing in Kyoto, and return on the Keihan Railway in the evening.

At Kuzuha Station, the atmosphere changes. People are returning from work. Students are coming home. Someone is cycling past the station, and other people are shopping for dinner.

You might stop at a supermarket yourself, buy something to eat, and walk back through the residential streets to The Showa House.

The next day might be Osaka. In the evening, you return to the same Kuzuha Station again.

After several days, streets that were completely unfamiliar can begin to look recognizable. “That is the road back to the house.” “I can stop at that supermarket on the way home.”

Nothing remarkable has happened. An ordinary town has simply become part of the trip.

You Are Not Staying Here to Sightsee in Hirakata

I am not suggesting that you visit Hirakata instead of Kyoto or Osaka.

Kyoto has things that can only be experienced in Kyoto. Osaka has its own attractions and character. Please enjoy them.

The role of Hirakata and Kuzuha is different. It is less a place you come to sightsee and more a place you return to after sightseeing.

By returning every evening, an ordinary town can gradually become part of the journey.

But There Is a Place Nearby That I Really Like

Even around an ordinary residential area, there are places nearby that I genuinely recommend. One of them is Iwashimizu Hachimangu Shrine.

It is easy to reach from Kuzuha on the Keihan Railway. The shrine is on Mount Otokoyama, and a cable car is available.

If you have the time and feel comfortable walking uphill, I personally recommend walking. I like the approach through the trees. The sounds of the town gradually fall away as you walk toward the shrine on the mountain.

Of course, taking the cable car is perfectly fine too. You do not need to climb the mountain just because I like walking.

Please check the shrine’s official website for current access and cable car information before visiting.

Sometimes the Places You Did Not Plan Become Memories

Before a trip, I plan famous sights, restaurants, transportation, and reservations. I imagine most travelers do something similar.

But after I return home, some memories come from things that were never important items on the itinerary: a supermarket, the road back to my accommodation, a shop I saw every morning, or a station I used several times.

It might also be an unexpected nearby place such as Iwashimizu Hachimangu. Later, I find myself thinking, “Ah, I liked that place.”

I like trips that leave room for memories like that.

Returning Somewhere That Is Neither Kyoto nor Osaka

If Kyoto is the main focus of a trip, staying in Kyoto can be more convenient. If Osaka is the main focus, staying in Osaka can be more convenient.

The practical question of choosing a base is covered in Best Base for Exploring Kyoto and Osaka. I also write about time and familiarity in How Many Days Do You Need for Kyoto, Osaka and Nara?

This article is about something else.

During the day, you walk through world-famous destinations. At night, you return to an ordinary Japanese residential neighbourhood.

You walk from the station, enter the house, take off your shoes, and relax in the tatami room. Perhaps you eat something bought at the local supermarket. Then you sleep and leave again the next morning.

These are ordinary actions. That is the point.

By the End of the Trip

You may not have known Kuzuha before coming to Japan. You probably did not travel to Japan because you wanted to visit Hirakata. That is completely fine.

But after several days, you may know which exit to take at Kuzuha Station, the road back to the house, where to buy a drink, and where the supermarket is.

You may have come to Japan to see Kyoto and Osaka, but by the time you leave, you may also remember the road from Kuzuha Station back to the house.

I think that kind of journey is interesting too.
