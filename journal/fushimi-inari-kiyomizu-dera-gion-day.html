KYOTO DAY TRIP · JULY 2026

How to Spend a Day at Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera & Gion

An early start, quiet streets, and a full day of Kyoto from The Showa House.

From Taka’s Notebook

Guests sometimes ask me, “If I only have one day in Kyoto, where would you recommend?”

Kyoto has far too many places to see everything in one day. But if I were leaving The Showa House for a day in Kyoto, I would probably start at Fushimi Inari, then continue toward Kiyomizu-dera, Yasaka Shrine, and Gion.

One reason is practical: the route works naturally with the Keihan Railway and walking. The other is that I enjoy walking between places instead of only moving from one famous attraction to another.

This is not the only way to spend a day in Kyoto. It is simply how I would spend it.

Begin at Fushimi Inari

From The Showa House, walk to Kuzuha Station and take the Keihan Main Line toward Fushimi-inari Station.

I like to visit Fushimi Inari early, partly because it becomes much busier later. It does not have to be six in the morning. I do not leave early so that I can finish sightseeing earlier; I leave early so that I do not have to hurry later.

Walk beneath the rows of vermilion torii gates and continue uphill if you feel like it. There is no need to complete the whole mountain route. If you want to walk farther, keep going. If you feel you have enjoyed enough, turn back.

On your way back down, stop in the small streets near the shrine for a light breakfast or a little shopping. The old-fashioned atmosphere here feels different from central Kyoto and is part of the pleasure of an early visit.

Continue to Kiyomizu-dera

From Fushimi-inari Station, take the Keihan Railway to Kiyomizu-Gojo Station, then walk uphill toward Kiyomizu-dera.

I think of this walk as part of the day, not only as the way from the station to the temple. The main approach can be lively, but step into a side street and Kyoto can become unexpectedly quiet again.

At the temple, I also recommend Tainai-meguri when it is available. This short experience takes you through a completely dark passage beneath Zuigu-do Hall. It feels very different from simply walking around the temple grounds.

Afterwards, continue through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka. If a shop looks interesting, stop. If you are hungry, eat. If you are tired, rest. You came to Kyoto to look at Kyoto, not to spend the whole day looking at your schedule.

Yasaka Shrine and Gion

When I walk from Kiyomizu-dera toward Gion, I naturally continue to Yasaka Shrine. It is famous, but it also has a personal meaning for me: my wife and I were married there, so it is one of the places in Kyoto that holds especially happy memories for me.

Perhaps that is why Yasaka Shrine always seems to appear when someone asks me where I would go in Kyoto.

From the shrine, continue naturally into Gion. There is no checklist to follow. Walk around, take a side street that interests you, or stop for tea. If you are tired, sit down.

Gion is usually busy, especially at popular times. To be honest, I get a little tired if I spend too long walking through crowds. If you find somewhere you like, it is perfectly fine to stay a little longer. You do not need to keep moving just because another famous place is waiting on the itinerary. When I have had enough of the crowd, the Kamo River begins to sound like a good idea.

Rest Beside the Kamo River

If you still have time and energy toward evening, walk toward the Kamo River. This is not another attraction to complete. It is where the pace of the day can change.

People in Kyoto sit along the riverbank talking, watching the water, or simply resting. One thing you may notice is how people tend to sit along the riverbank at almost regular intervals. Even that has become a familiar and slightly amusing Kyoto scene.

After walking all day, you do not need another attraction. Just sitting beside the river is enough. I like the idea of ending a Kyoto day this way.

You Don’t Have to Finish the Route

Perhaps you stayed longer at Fushimi Inari. Perhaps you found an interesting shop near Kiyomizu-dera, sat in a café longer than expected, or are simply tired. That is fine. Skip the Kamo River and return home if you want. If you still feel energetic, keep walking.

A model itinerary should help you imagine a possible day, not tell you exactly how you must spend it.

Returning to The Showa House

Whenever you feel ready, take the Keihan Railway back toward Kuzuha. Walk the familiar road from Kuzuha Station to The Showa House, take off your shoes, and relax in the tatami room.

Dinner can be something you bought near Kuzuha Station or at a local supermarket, or perhaps you already ate in Kyoto. The next day might be Osaka or Uji, or it might simply be a slower day.

I would not try to conquer Kyoto in one day. Start with Fushimi Inari in the morning, then walk. Stop somewhere if it interests you, rest if you are tired, and when you have had enough, come home. That is the kind of Kyoto day I like.
