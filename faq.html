FAQ

Everything you need to know before your stay.

Is The Showa House a hotel?

No.

The Showa House is a traditional Japanese home, not a hotel.

We welcome only one group at a time, so you can enjoy a quiet stay while experiencing everyday life in Japan.

Why do I need to take off my shoes?

Removing shoes before entering a home is a long-standing custom in Japan.

It helps keep living spaces, especially tatami rooms, clean and comfortable.

Can I cook?

Yes.

The kitchen is available during your stay.

Basic cookware, dishes, and utensils are provided.

Is the neighborhood safe?

Yes.

The Showa House is located in a quiet residential neighborhood in Hirakata, between Kyoto and Osaka.

You can enjoy the calm atmosphere of everyday life in Japan.

Is Wi-Fi available?

Yes.

Free Wi-Fi is available throughout the house.

How far is the nearest station?

Kuzuha Station on the Keihan Railway is about a 10-minute walk away.

If you have large luggage or the weather is bad, taxis are also available from the taxi stand in front of the station.

Can I leave my luggage before check-in?

Please contact us in advance.

We will do our best to help if possible.

How many guests can stay?

The Showa House accommodates up to two guests.

Is the bathroom shared?

No.

The bathroom is for your group’s private use.

Do the hosts live in the house?

No.

The hosts live in a house nearby.

During your stay, The Showa House is reserved for your group, so you can relax and enjoy your privacy.

Can children stay?

Yes.

Please note that this is a traditional Japanese house with wooden stairs and some original features.

Young children should be supervised by an adult.

Can I smoke?

No.

Smoking is not permitted inside the house.

Is parking available?

Yes.

Free parking is available.

Please let us know in advance if you plan to arrive by car.

How can I make a reservation?

First-time reservations are accepted through Airbnb.

Please use the “View on Airbnb” button to check availability and make a reservation.

Can you help me with my trip in Japan?

Of course.

If you have questions about Japanese customs, transportation, or places to visit, please feel free to ask us.

We will be happy to help whenever possible.
