A Week at The Showa House

One house, different days, and a slower way to explore Kansai.

From Taka’s Notebook

If I had a week in one place, I probably would not stay in a different hotel every night.

I would think about where I wanted to go, choose one convenient place, and stay there for several days if I could. A station I did not know on the first day would begin to feel familiar. A road I first walked while looking at a map would soon become one I could follow without checking.

I would learn where the supermarket was and think, “I will buy dinner here tonight.” I might find a discounted bento or a food I liked enough to buy again. I enjoy that feeling when I travel.

During a week at The Showa House, you can go to Kyoto, Osaka, and Uji, while also leaving room for a slower day. This is not a seven-day schedule you need to follow. It is simply the kind of week I might choose for myself.

Day 1

Get to Know the House and the Neighborhood

After you arrive, leave your luggage, take a little time to rest, and get to know the house.

If you feel like going outside, you could look around Kuzuha Station and KUZUHA MALL, find the supermarket, or walk through the neighborhood. You might buy dinner and bring it back to The Showa House.

If you arrive around noon and still have time and energy, Iwashimizu Hachimangu is another possibility. I have described that kind of afternoon in Arriving Around Noon? A Relaxed First Afternoon Near The Showa House.

If you are tired, you do not need to go anywhere. Resting in the tatami room is also a good way to begin.

Day 2

An Early Day in Kyoto

I like to start early on a Kyoto day. From Kuzuha, take the Keihan Railway toward Fushimi Inari and begin among the torii gates before the busiest part of the day.

From there, you could continue to Kiyomizu-dera, walk through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, and then carry on toward Yasaka Shrine and Gion. The Kamo River is a good place to sit when you have walked enough.

This does not need to be a checklist. Stop at a shop if it interests you, rest when you are tired, and leave something for another day. My How to Spend a Day at Fushimi Inari, Kiyomizu-dera & Gion article follows this route in the same unhurried way.

When you have had enough of Kyoto, take the Keihan Railway back to Kuzuha and return to the same house.

Day 3

Osaka Can Start a Little Later

I enjoy Kyoto in the morning, but Osaka is a city I am happy to visit later in the day. The afternoon and evening are interesting there too.

You might choose Osaka Castle and Nakanoshima, spend time around Umeda, or go toward Namba and Dotonbori. I would not try to fit all of them into one day.

Stay for dinner if you want, then take the train back to Kuzuha. Yesterday was Kyoto and today was Osaka, but the house you return to has not changed.

After a few days, arriving at Kuzuha Station may already bring a small feeling of relief.

Day 4

A Day Without Trying to Do Too Much

After three days of travel, you may be tired. Sleep a little later, walk around the neighborhood, or stay in the house until noon.

If the weather is good and you would like a walk, Sewaritei is only two stops from Kuzuha. A Walk at Sewaritei, Just Two Stops from Kuzuha explains why I like it even when the cherry trees are not in bloom.

You do not need to make it into a full sightseeing day. Walk beside the river for a while, turn back when you feel like it, and return to Kuzuha.

Walking around KUZUHA MALL or buying dinner at the supermarket can be enough. A journey can include a day that feels a little like a day off. Having a week makes room for that.

Day 5

Uji

If you like matcha, I would make one day for Uji. From Kuzuha, take the Keihan Main Line to Chushojima, then change to the Keihan Uji Line.

You could visit Byodoin, have tea or matcha, and walk beside the Uji River. If you still have time and energy, Koshoji is another option.

I explain the journey in How to Get to Uji by Keihan Railway.

Uji and the Keihan Uji Line also appear in Sound! Euphonium, a Japanese anime about a high school concert band. Many fans visit places they recognize from the story. I am a fan too, although you do not need to know the anime to enjoy Uji.

Day 6

Go Back Somewhere, or Decide Tomorrow

By now, you may want to return to somewhere in Kyoto. There may be a place in Osaka you did not have time for, or you may have decided that you would like to see Nara.

You may also discover that you do not want to go far at all.

You do not need to decide all seven days before the journey begins. I prefer to let the plan change with my interests, energy, and the weather, instead of forcing myself to follow a schedule I made before I arrived.

Decide this day when you know what you want from it.

Day 7

The Familiar Road One More Time

On the first day, you did not know the road to Kuzuha Station. Now you may know which entrance to use, where the supermarket is, and how to walk back to The Showa House without looking at a map.

A week does not make you a local. It can, however, give you a few familiar places in a town that was unknown when you arrived.

The temples of Kyoto and the streets of Osaka will be part of the journey. Later, you may also remember the road from Kuzuha Station, the supermarket you visited more than once, or the time you spent resting in the tatami room.

I think those ordinary memories are part of travel too.

One House, Many Different Days

Staying at The Showa House for a week does not mean spending a week sightseeing in Hirakata.

There can be a day in Kyoto, a day in Osaka, a day in Uji, and a day when you walk no farther than the neighborhood. The days are different, but you return to the same house and leave your suitcase where it is.

After a while, you learn the road to the station and the location of the supermarket. The place becomes a little more familiar.

You cannot see everything in one week, but you do not need to. See a few places slowly, rest when you are tired, and go out again tomorrow.

That is the kind of week I would choose.
